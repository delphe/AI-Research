Art by Levi Smith

As a reader of topics like these, I often look at the conclusion first. To save you from scrolling down…

Conclusion:

Can you have an ethically sourced, sustainably grown, free-range, GMO-free, locally sourced, artisan AI? No! There’s a ‘vegan but bacon’ principle I recently heard about that puts things into perspective, though. It’s the idea that someone may want to be vegan, but they can’t because they love bacon too much. That’s okay! You don’t have to be perfect. You can be as close to vegan as possible while also eating bacon. The same goes for AI and many other things. You can use AI, knowing how bad it can be for the environment, while trying your best to use it as sustainably as possible.

To replace sites like ChatGPT with a more eco-friendly option, as a regular user, I’d recommend checking out EcoGPT, Ecosia AI, and Viro AI, in that order. If you’re tech-savvy and can power your own systems with renewable energy, I’d recommend running local AI models and trying to reduce your reliance on cloud solutions. You just need enough memory and processing power, which all depend on what tasks you want AI to do.

Overview:

There’s a division amongst people regarding the use of AI. Some praise its efficiency and usefulness, while others absolutely hate it. There are many reasons to dislike AI.

Ethical Reasons: Many models are trained on stolen data.

Replacing Humans: Layoffs have already occurred at many companies as work normally done by humans is replaced by AI.

Possibly AI-generated. Artistic credit: South Park

Sycophantic Behavior: AI platforms want you to keep asking questions and draw you in. What better way than to stroke your ego and let you think you’re right most of the time?

Environmental Impact: The amount of water it takes to cool data centers and the amount of energy it takes to power them is enormous!

I’m going to focus on the environmental impact and evaluate some AI options that claim to be eco-friendly. I used to hate AI, but my fear of it taking over my job made me want to understand it more and make sure I knew how to use it. If I’m going to use AI, I want to find a way to use it sustainably.

Claude isn’t an environmentally friendly option, but it’s one I used the most because it isn’t as sycophantic as many others. For comparison, I’ll start with Claude and branch out from there, using AI platforms that claim to be eco-friendly. My prompts for each AI will be shared as links, and you’ll be able to see how they each respond.
