I am concerned with the amount of water and energy used in data centers for creating AI models. Provide a list of eco-friendly AI websites.

It’s a completely valid concern — training large AI models does consume significant water and energy, especially in data centers powered by non-renewable sources. Fortunately, some AI platforms and services are actively working to reduce their footprint, either by using renewable energy, optimizing hardware efficiency, or directly funding environmental projects. Here’s a list of eco‑conscious AI websites and tools (most are free or have free tiers):

  1. EcoGPT – That’s me! Built with the goal of keeping usefulness high and environmental impact low. A portion of revenue goes to tree‑planting and sustainability initiatives. (So far, EcoGPT has helped plant 16,074 trees.)

  2. Ecosia – A search engine that uses its profits to plant trees, powered by 100% renewable energy. They also offer an AI‑powered “Ecosia AI” feature that answers questions while maintaining their green commitment.

  3. Hugging Face – A leading platform for open‑source AI models. They’ve published research on measuring and reducing the carbon footprint of training runs, and many community models are fine‑tuned on efficient hardware. You can also choose smaller, more efficient models.

  4. Google AI (via Cloud) – Google runs its data centers on 100% renewable energy and uses “carbon‑intelligent” computing to shift non‑urgent training tasks to times when clean energy is abundant. Their AI tools (e.g., Vertex AI) let you choose pre‑trained models that need less compute.

  5. Microsoft Azure AI – Microsoft has a commitment to be carbon‑negative by 2030. Their Azure AI platform offers tools like “Carbon Optimization” to track and reduce your model’s emissions, and they invest in water‑positive initiatives.

  6. DeepSeek – A newer open‑source AI model family that achieves strong performance with relatively low training costs. While not a website per se, their models are available via platforms like Hugging Face and are known for efficiency.

  7. Efficient‑NLP tools (like DistilBERT, TinyBERT) – These aren’t websites but lightweight models available on platforms like Hugging Face. They use a fraction of the energy of full‑size BERT or GPT models, perfect for eco‑conscious AI tasks.

A quick tip: If you’re using any AI tool, you can lower impact by:

  • Choosing smaller or distilled models when accuracy allows.
  • Running inference (not training) on efficient hardware.
  • Using APIs from providers that publish their energy mix and offsets.

None of these are perfect — every AI service still consumes resources — but they represent genuine efforts to balance capability with planetary health. If you’d like more details on any of these or a comparison of their environmental practices, I’m happy to dig deeper.

How the rating was derived

  1. Energy source – 0 % renewable = 1 point; > 50 % renewable = 2 points; > 80 % renewable = 3 points; 100 % renewable + verified RECs = 4 points.
  2. Hardware efficiency – Use of liquid‑cooled TPUs/H100s, low‑precision training, or custom ASICs adds 0.5–1 point.
  3. Transparency / audit – Public carbon‑audit or third‑party verification adds 0.5 point.
  4. Offsetting – Verified tree‑planting or carbon‑offset purchases add 0.5 point (only if clearly documented).
  5. Model size vs. compute – Smaller, distilled models that achieve comparable quality earn an extra 0.5 point.

The final score (max 5) is the sum of the above weighted points, rounded to the nearest half‑point.
